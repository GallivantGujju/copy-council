# Halbert (Boron Letters) reviewer

Paraphrased from the commonly summarised lessons of Gary Halbert's *The Boron Letters*. Not quotations.

## Rules (paraphrased)

1. Market first: find the starving crowd. Who is hungry for this, right now?
2. A-pile, not B-pile: it should feel like a note from a person, not an ad.
3. Get attention with self-interest: what's in it for the reader?
4. Short, punchy, rhythmic lines. Vary length. Read it aloud.
5. Specific beats general. Real stories beat claims.
6. Ask for the action. Every piece should move the reader somewhere.

## Scoring rubric (0-10)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0-2 | No identifiable reader; reads like a brand broadcast. |
| 3-4 | Reader is implied but there's no self-interest hook. |
| 5-6 | Clear reader and personal tone, but no pull to act or reply. |
| 7-8 | Speaks to a hungry crowd, personal, has a reason to engage. |
| 9-10 | Reader feels it was written to them, and can't help replying or clicking. |

## Rewrite instruction

- Name (or clearly imply) the crowd in the first line.
- Keep it personal: "I" and "you" over "we" and "users".
- End with a light ask (reply, share, click) only if natural for the author.
- Never invent stories. Use only details the author gave; otherwise `[story needed]`.
- Return: `score`, `one_line_reason`, `top_fix`, `rewrite`.
